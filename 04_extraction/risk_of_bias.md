# Risk-of-Bias Assessment for *Acropora palmata* Demographic Synthesis

**Adapted Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for observational vital rate compilations**

Date: 2026-03-26

---

## Scoring Criteria

Each study is scored on 5 domains (0 = high risk, 1 = moderate risk, 2 = low risk):

| Domain | Score 2 (Low Risk) | Score 1 (Moderate Risk) | Score 0 (High Risk) |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Selection** | Random/census sampling | Convenience but described | Unclear or biased |
| **Measurement** | Tagged + measured repeatedly | Photographed or estimated from figures | Unclear method |
| **Attrition** | <10% lost to follow-up | 10--30% lost | >30% lost or not reported |
| **Mortality definition** | Explicit definition | Implied but reasonable | Ambiguous or non-standard |
| **Temporal adequacy** | >=1 yr direct annual | <1 yr or >3 yr requiring annualization | Cross-sectional or very short |

## Interpretation

The overall risk-of-bias profile for this synthesis is **moderate, with strengths in measurement and temporal adequacy but weaknesses in selection and attrition**.

**Measurement quality is generally strong** (mean 1.68/2). Most studies used tagged, individually tracked colonies with repeated measurements of size (L x W x %Live or equivalent). The NOAA survey, Vardi dissertation, and Rogers & Muller (2012) represent particularly rigorous measurement protocols with repeat photography, GPS relocation, and standardized size metrics. The weakest measurement scores come from studies that relied on visual estimation of tissue cover without direct measurement (Bruckner & Bruckner 2001, Maurer et al. 2022, Fundemar).

**Selection is the weakest domain** (mean 1.28/2). Only 4 of 17 studies used truly random or census-based sampling (NOAA, Neely, Vardi, Rogers & Muller). The remaining 13 studies used convenience or experimental designs -- fragments selected from donor colonies, nursery stock, or storm-generated material. This is inherent to the mix of natural population monitoring and restoration studies in this synthesis, and it means that the individual-level data are dominated by fragments/outplants in small size classes and NOAA census data in large size classes.

**Attrition is the most problematic domain** (mean 1.00/2). Three studies scored 0 (Bruckner & Bruckner 2001, Garrison & Ward 2008, Sutherland et al. 2016), reflecting either high fragment loss, colony disappearance, or extreme biological mortality. However, for studies of a declining species experiencing disease outbreaks and hurricanes, high observed mortality is not methodological bias but genuine biological signal. The distinction between "lost to follow-up" (methodological) and "died" (biological) is blurred in coral demography, since colony disappearance (no skeleton found) could represent either. Studies that explicitly distinguished these fates (Garrison & Ward, Sutherland et al.) received credit for methodological rigor even when biological attrition was high.

**Mortality definition quality is adequate** (mean 1.53/2). Studies that explicitly defined colony death (no tissue/skeleton gone; 100% tissue loss; DOA) scored 2. The main concern is Kuffner et al. (2020), which used a non-standard threshold of >=50% tissue loss as the mortality criterion, potentially inflating apparent mortality relative to other studies. Several restoration studies scored 1 because mortality was implied from alive/dead status without a formal definition of partial vs. whole-colony mortality.

**Temporal adequacy is generally good** (mean 1.63/2). Most studies provided direct annual or multi-year survival data. Studies scoring 1 had sub-annual monitoring periods (Williams & Miller 2010: 44 weeks; Kuffner et al. 2020: ~6-month intervals; USGS USVI: half-year intervals) or required annualization from longer multi-year records. The Muller et al. (2008) study, while 2.5 years long with monthly data, also required some annualization for the survival rates used in meta-analysis.

**Key caveats for the synthesis:**

1. **NOAA dominance**: The highest-quality study (10/10) is the largest single contributor to individual-level data. Results are heavily weighted toward this data source, though the addition of Neely et al. 2022 (878 colonies) has partially reduced NOAA's dominance.

2. **Tier structure matters**: The 7 Tier 1 (individual-level) studies average 7.1/10, while the 11 Tier 2 (summary/expanded search) studies average 6.8/10. The Tier 2 studies are not systematically lower quality, but their data contribute less statistical weight due to summary-level reporting.

3. **Mortality definition heterogeneity**: The single biggest methodological concern is that "mortality" means different things across studies. NOAA defines it as no tissue/skeleton remaining; Kuffner et al. use >=50% tissue loss; Garrison & Ward include colony disappearance. This heterogeneity likely contributes to the extreme I-squared of 96.6% observed in the meta-analysis.

4. **No study scored 0 on selection**: Even restoration/experimental studies described their selection methods adequately. This is reassuring for transparency but does not eliminate the fundamental non-representativeness of fragment-based studies for inferring wild population vital rates.

5. **One study at high risk**: Only Bruckner & Bruckner (2001) scored in the moderate--high range (4/10), primarily due to high attrition and less precise measurement methods. Its contribution to the meta-analysis should be interpreted cautiously.

## Study-Level Assessments

### Tier 1: Individual-Level Data

**1. NOAA_survey (10/10, Low Risk)**
- Selection: Census of all tagged colonies within permanent monitoring plots across Florida Keys, Curacao, and Navassa (Score: 2)
- Measurement: Quarterly measurement of L x W x H x %Live for each tagged colony, with snail counts, disease/bleaching/mortality ranks (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Minimal methodological attrition; colonies tracked over 20 years with clear documentation of mortality events (Score: 2)
- Mortality definition: Explicit -- "colonies considered dead if skeleton found with no tissue OR no skeleton/tissue remaining" (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: Quarterly surveys from 2004--2024; annual intervals directly available (Score: 2)

**2. pausch_et_al_2018 (8/10, Low Risk)**
- Selection: Experimental design with fragments from donor genets assigned to treatments; well-described but not random from wild population (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Tagged fragments with L, W, H, proportion_live measured at each survey (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Status categories include "Missing" -- some fragment loss over 2-year study, estimated 10--15% (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Status recorded as OK, Dead, or Missing; Dead explicitly defined (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: ~1 year intervals between May 2014 and 2016 surveys (Score: 2)

**3. kuffner_et_al_2020 (6/10, Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Nursery-reared fragments outplanted; convenience sample (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Buoyant weight, surface area change, height change measured with high precision (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Marine heatwave in 2014 caused substantial mortality; some fragment loss (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Non-standard -- >=50% tissue loss classified as "dead" (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: 3 time intervals over ~1.5 years; individual intervals are ~6 months (Score: 1)

**4. USGS_USVI_exp (7/10, Low--Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Outplanted fragments at 3 sites; experimental design (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Calcification rate, surface area change, height change measured with precision instruments (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Some fragment mortality between intervals; condition tracked (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Condition recorded as alive/dead; clear binary (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: Half-year intervals (June 2019 -- August 2021); annualization needed (Score: 1)

**5. fundemar_fragments (5/10, Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Nursery fragments from 6 Dominican Republic localities; convenience (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Size data present but limited; only Table 1 has full annual coverage (Score: 1)
- Attrition: Incomplete temporal coverage for most tables; some recruits added post-outplanting had to be removed (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Implied from monitoring; not explicitly stated (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: Table 1 has 12-month data; Tables 2--6 only 5 months; recruit data from 5--16 months (Score: 1)

**6. mendoza_quiroz_et_al_2023 (5/10, Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Nursery and outplant fragments; experimental restoration design (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Diameter measurements for nursery/Cuevones; L x W for Picudas; heterogeneous metrics (Score: 1)
- Attrition: Some attrition not fully quantified; survival curves show losses (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Implied from survival counts at each survey (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: Variable monitoring periods (6--12 months); some need annualization (Score: 1)

### Tier 2: Hand-Extracted Summary Data

**7. vardi_2011 (9/10, Low Risk)**
- Selection: 15 permanent plots across 5 reefs, chosen for minimum A. palmata density; near-census within plots (Score: 2)
- Measurement: All colonies measured annually with L x W x %Live; standardized projected surface area metric (Score: 2)
- Attrition: 21% decline in abundance (340 to 269) over 6 years; some colony disappearance (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Explicit size-class-specific mortality rates reported; matrix model framework (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: Annual surveys 2004--2010; 6 annual transition matrices constructed directly (Score: 2)

**8. bruckner_bruckner_2001 (4/10, Moderate--High Risk)**
- Selection: Fragments at ship grounding restoration site; convenience (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Visual estimation of tissue cover; photographic documentation but measurements less standardized (Score: 1)
- Attrition: High -- substantial storm damage and fragment loss; 57% survived 2 years but many fragments displaced (Score: 0)
- Mortality definition: Implied from condition assessments (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: 2-year follow-up; annualization required from biennial data (Score: 1)

- Measurement: 357--401 fragments tagged and measured (length, width, surface area) repeatedly; well-documented size distributions (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Fragment loss documented but within moderate range; substrate type and condition tracked (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Explicit -- fragment dead = no living tissue; partial mortality quantified separately (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: 1--3 year follow-up after Hurricane Georges (1998); annual rates directly estimable (Score: 2)

**11. forrester_et_al_2013 (6/10, Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Fragment outplanting experiment in BVI; convenience (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Fragments monitored but measurement detail limited in publication (Score: 1)
- Attrition: Some fragment displacement and loss (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Implied from survival assessments (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: Annual monitoring period (Score: 2)

**12. rosales_et_al_2024 (8/10, Low Risk)**
- Selection: Large-scale Florida Keys restoration fragments; genotyped (Score: 1)
- Measurement: L, W, H, %Live recorded; LiveFrag binary outcome clearly coded; raw data publicly available (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Some fragment mortality tracked in raw data; moderate attrition (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Explicit -- LiveFrag coded 0/1 at each survey; Adjusted Area calculated (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: Multi-year monitoring with annual survey intervals (Score: 2)

**13. maurer_et_al_2022 (7/10, Low--Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Nursery fragments from nearby wild reefs; genotyped; randomized placement on lines (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Visual inspection of alive/dead status only; no individual size measurements (initial size assumed ~5x5 cm) (Score: 1)
- Attrition: Excellent -- only 2 of 128 corals physically lost over 3 years (1.6%) (Score: 2)
- Mortality definition: Alive or dead recorded at each visit; implied but clear binary (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: 3-year study with annual survival assessments (Score: 2)

**14. williams_miller_2010 (5/10, Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: 54 naturally occurring fragments on Amanda's Reef, Biscayne NP; convenience but described (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Photographed; length and tissue cover visually estimated; performance ranked qualitatively (1--3) (Score: 1)
- Attrition: 9 fragments physically lost + 1 complete mortality = 10 of 54 (18.5%) (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Complete mortality or physical loss; implied from ranking system (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: 44-week study (< 1 year); requires annualization (Score: 1)

**15. garrison_ward_2008 (7/10, Low--Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Storm-generated fragments transplanted to recipient reefs + control colonies matched by size/depth; well-designed but non-random (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Tagged, photographed, sketched, and measured at 6-month then annual intervals; maximum linear dimension used as size metric for A. palmata (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Very high -- 75% mortality/disappearance over 5 years; 58% disappeared (Score: 0)
- Mortality definition: Explicit -- "dead and removed from further inclusion if: (1) entire colony/fragment disappeared and could not be relocated, or (2) live tissue was not observed (100% tissue loss)" (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: 5-year study (1999--2004) with 6-month then annual assessments; direct annual survival estimable (Score: 2)

### Tier 2: Expanded Search Summary Data

**16. rogers_muller_2012 (9/10, Low Risk)**
- Selection: Census of all 69 A. palmata colonies within a 17,627 m2 reef zone at Haulover Bay (Score: 2)
- Measurement: Monthly photography + annual volume estimation (H x W x L); disease/bleaching/mortality documented in detail (Score: 2)
- Attrition: 25 of 69 (36.2%) died over 7 years -- biological mortality, not methodological loss; all deaths documented (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Explicit -- complete colony mortality = no live tissue; partial mortality documented separately; causes of death attributed (disease, bleaching, physical damage, unknown) (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: 7-year monthly monitoring (2003--2009) with annual size measurements through 2010 (Score: 2)

**17. ramos_romero_et_al_2025 (7/10, Low--Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Fragments collected from randomly selected colonies at each reef crest; experimental design across 4 crests (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Vernier caliper measurements (width, height) at 3--4 time points; Kaplan-Meier survival analysis (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Some fragments died, detached, or not found; varied by crest (moderate, 10--30% range) (Score: 1)
- Mortality definition: Fragment dead or missing; implied from KM survival curves (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: Monitoring over 14--15 months at each crest; approximately annual (Score: 2)

**18. muller_et_al_2008 (8/10, Low Risk)**
- Selection: 60 colonies chosen using random number-generating algorithm in GIS within mapped reef area at Hawksnest Bay (Score: 2)
- Measurement: Monthly photography with scale; recent mortality traced and measured using ImageJ; disease prevalence quantified (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Only 5 of 60 colonies died during the 2.5-year study (8.3% total mortality); very low (Score: 2)
- Mortality definition: Partial mortality = area of recent tissue loss measured; complete mortality observed but disease caused partial not whole-colony death in most cases (Score: 1)
- Temporal adequacy: 2.5-year study (May 2004 -- Dec 2006); disease-focused, survival requires some annualization (Score: 1)

**19. sutherland_et_al_2016 (7/10, Low--Moderate Risk)**
- Selection: Historical: all A. palmata at EDR photostation (n = 92); Contemporary: all colonies at 7 FKNMS reefs (n = 126); near-census within study areas (Score: 1)
- Measurement: Long-term photographic monitoring (1994--2014); digital image analysis with ImageJ; lesion areas quantified (Score: 2)
- Attrition: Extreme biological loss -- 91 of 92 historical colonies lost at EDR (97.8% decline over 10 years); 56 of 126 contemporary colonies lost (44.4%). Primarily biological, not methodological (Score: 0)
- Mortality definition: Explicit three-category system: DOA (dead skeleton visible), TKO (colony physically removed), FUS (fusion of distinct colonies) (Score: 2)
- Temporal adequacy: 20-year study with annual/seasonal surveys; direct annual survival estimable (Score: 2)
