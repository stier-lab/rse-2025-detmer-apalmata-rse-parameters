## DHW Values for Major Bleaching Years

### Florida Keys

| Year | Event | CRW Satellite DHW | In-situ DHW | Survival in our data |
|------|-------|-------------------|-------------|---------------------|
| 2005 | Mass bleaching | 5.0-9.7 (varies inshore/offshore) | — | NOAA ~88% |
| 2010 | Cold snap + warm season | 3.2 (warm season) | — | Neely 78% |
| 2014 | Major bleaching | 4.5-5.3 | **7.5-7.7** (Williams et al. 2017) | **Neely 53%** |
| 2015 | Repeat bleaching | 4.1-5.4 | **9.5-10.9** (Williams et al. 2017) | Neely 61%, Pausch 52% |
| 2017 | Hurricane Irma | 1.6 | — | NOAA 66% (physical damage) |
| 2023 | Record heatwave | **20.4** | ~22 (Precht et al. 2025) | **NOAA 54%** |

**Critical:** Satellite underestimates inshore heat stress by 1.5-2x (Williams et al. 2017). In-situ DHW at Cheeca Rocks was 7.7 in 2014 vs satellite 5.3.

## Key Findings for the Synthesis

1. **Satellite DHW underestimates reef-level heat stress** — inshore reefs heat 1.5-2x more than the 5km satellite pixel captures. Our DHW values are conservative.

2. **The 2023 FL Keys event was off the scale** — DHW=20.4, ~4x the 2014 event. Precht et al. (2025, Science) documented **functional extinction of Acropora from Florida**. Our NOAA data from 2023 (54% survival) captures this catastrophe.

3. **Curacao 2009 low survival (55%) was NOT heat-driven** (DHW=0.8) — other factors (disease, predation, storms) caused the mortality at Boca Santa Marta.

4. **Neely 2014 catastrophe at DHW=7.7 (in-situ)** was amplified by *Coralliophila* predation, explaining why moderate DHW caused major mortality.
