# Recruitment and Fecundity Scope Note

This project contains some direct life-history evidence relevant to reproduction and early life stages, but it does not yet support a full demographic model of recruitment or fecundity across the Caribbean. The current synthesis is strongest for survival, growth, size thresholds, and disturbance context.

## What is directly supported

- A maturity threshold is available: colonies around 4,000 cm² are associated with high probability of gamete production in the current literature synthesis.
- There is evidence that partial mortality reduces reproductive output, including reduced egg volume in colonies with lesions.
- Several recruitment-related bottlenecks are documented, including temperature-sensitive settlement success and high early post-settlement mortality.
- Restoration-outplant literature provides some direct context for when sexually mature outplants can begin contributing to reproduction.

## What exists as contextual literature evidence

- Habitat-process evidence that affects reproduction or recruitment but is not itself a direct fecundity rate, such as grazing pressure, macroalgal cover, pollution, and reef-framework loss.
- Disturbance-linked context that likely affects recruitment or fecundity indirectly, including bleaching, hurricanes, disease outbreaks, and chronic heat or eutrophication pressure.
- Metabolic or physiological measurements that help interpret performance, but do not translate cleanly into a population fecundity parameter.

## What is not yet estimable from the current synthesis

- A Caribbean-wide fecundity schedule by size class.
- A direct recruitment rate that can be compared across all regions and study types.
- A disturbance-adjusted reproductive output model linking adult condition, gamete production, settlement, and post-settlement survival in one framework.
- Age-specific fecundity or lifetime reproductive output with enough replication to support robust inference.
- A population-level estimate of how much recruitment contributes to `lambda` relative to adult survival and growth.

## Manuscript implication

Reproduction and recruitment should be presented as constrained supporting evidence rather than as fully modeled demographic rates. The safest manuscript claim is that the literature identifies clear reproductive thresholds and strong early-life bottlenecks, but the available data are not yet sufficient to estimate a full Caribbean recruitment-fecundity module with the same confidence as survival and growth.

## Bottom line

The current synthesis can say what makes colonies reproductively plausible, what makes recruits vulnerable, and where thermal or disturbance stress likely suppresses early life history. It cannot yet claim a complete, regionally transferable fecundity model.
