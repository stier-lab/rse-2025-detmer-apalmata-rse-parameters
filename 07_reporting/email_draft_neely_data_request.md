Hi Karen,

I hope this finds you well. I think we may have crossed paths years ago — if I'm remembering right, you were a TA for one of the summer courses at Duke Marine Lab back when I was a student there. I'm not sure if you'd remember, but it's a small world in coral ecology!

I'm writing because my graduate student Raine Detmer and I are working on a synthesis of *Acropora palmata* demographic data across the Caribbean. We're building an updated population viability assessment using a Lefkovitch projection model, pulling together survival and growth data from every published study we can find. The manuscript is targeting *Coral Reefs* and follows PRISMA systematic review guidelines.

Your 2022 paper in Frontiers ("Population trajectory and stressors of *Acropora palmata* sites in the Florida Keys") is exactly the kind of work we've been looking for. The 508 tagged colonies you tracked across 9 sites over 5 years represent an incredible dataset — and critically, the sites you monitored (Lower Keys, Sombrero, Biscayne NP, Dry Tortugas) fill a major geographic gap in our synthesis. All of our current Florida data comes from the NOAA Upper Keys monitoring program, so your sites would be the first representation of these other sub-regions.

We were wondering if you'd be willing to share the colony-level data from that study. What we'd need is fairly simple:

- **Per colony:** alive/dead status at each annual survey (or the final fate)
- **Colony size:** L, W, H measurements (or the SA/LAI you calculated)
- **Site identity** for each colony

We would of course credit your contribution fully — either as a data citation or co-authorship, whatever feels appropriate to you. We're also happy to share our full dataset and analysis pipeline in return.

Our current meta-analysis has 20 study-level effects from 16 unique studies across 11 Caribbean regions. Your data would be one of the largest single additions and would substantially strengthen the analysis.
