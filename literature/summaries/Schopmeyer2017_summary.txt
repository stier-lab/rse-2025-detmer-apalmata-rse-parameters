Schopmeyer SA, Lirman D, Bartels E, Gilliam DS, Goergen EA, Griffin SP,
Johnson ME, Lustic C, Maxwell K, Walter CS. 2017. Regional restoration
benchmarks for Acropora cervicornis. Coral Reefs 36: 1047-1057.
DOI: 10.1007/s00338-017-1596-3.

ROLE IN DETMER-STIER SYNTHESIS
==============================
Sister-species reference paper. NOT an A. palmata data source — excluded
from extraction at full-text screen on species grounds.

Used in manuscript Introduction and Discussion as the canonical A. cervicornis
regional restoration benchmark against which we test whether origin effects
(nursery ex-situ vs. in-situ, nursery vs. outplant, natural vs. restoration)
generalize across acroporids.

STUDY DESIGN
============
- 6 Caribbean A. cervicornis restoration programs (Nova Southeastern, UMiami,
  FWC, Mote Marine, TNC Dry Tortugas, NOAA Puerto Rico)
- 13 nurseries, 161 genotypes, 20,316 nursery corals, 36,507 outplanted corals
- Monitoring: 1-2 yr post-fragmentation for donor colonies, 1 yr in nursery,
  1-2 yr after outplant
- Data collection period: 2010-2014
- Excluded disturbance years — benchmarks describe "normal" conditions

KEY QUANTITATIVE FINDINGS (A. cervicornis, non-disturbance)
===========================================================
Donor colonies (12 mo after 3-4 branch tips collected):
  - Mean live tissue cover: 85.0 ± 5.8% (range across regions)
  - Only 5.1% of donors experienced complete mortality
  - No significant mortality or growth differences vs. control branches
    (MIA: donor growth 6.9 ± 0.4 cm/yr vs. control 6.2 ± 0.7 cm/yr, p=0.337)
  - Causes of tissue loss: breakage (4.2%), predation (2.9%), algal/
    sponge/hydroid competition (2.1%)

Nursery fragment survival (12 mo in situ):
  - All regions combined: 90.8 ± 4.5% (range 84.8-96.0%)
  - n = 1,456 corals, 85 genotypes

Nursery growth and productivity:
  - Growth (TLE): 10.5-29.5 cm/yr among regions; overall mean 17.8 ± 7.8
  - Productivity (growth/initial TLE): 2.6-6.7 cm/yr on blocks (FL);
    52.5 ± 28.8 on floating arrays (PR) - significantly higher, p<0.001
  - Overall nursery productivity benchmark: 4.3 ± 0.8 cm/yr on blocks

Outplant survival (12 mo after transplantation):
  - All regions combined: 85.2 ± 9.7% (range 74.7-93.1%)
  - n across 5 regions: 933 corals, 60 genotypes, 25 sites
  - 24-mo survival (3 FL regions): BC 66.7%, MIA 79.7%, MK 78.7%
  - Within-region site variation is large: 1 MIA site had >90% mortality
    due to Hermodice/Coralliophila predation (p < 0.001 vs. 11 other sites)
  - Causes of tissue loss: breakage (23.6%), predation (12.5%)

Outplant growth/productivity:
  - Growth: 25.6-80.6 cm/yr TLE across regions; overall 46.2 ± 20.8
  - Productivity: 2.6-11.2 cm/yr; overall mean 4.9 ± 3.6
  - Outplant productivity slightly HIGHER than nursery

PROPOSED BENCHMARKS (first year of A. cervicornis restoration)
==============================================================
1. >75% live tissue cover on donor colonies
2. >80% survivorship of nursery corals
3. >70% survivorship of outplanted corals
4. <=10% of biomass collected from healthy large donor colonies

Stop-light framework (applies to each benchmark):
  GREEN: within 10% of overall mean -> no action required
  YELLOW: 10-20% below mean -> caution, minor adjustments
  RED: >20% below mean -> action required (improve methods/design/siting)

Framework only applies in non-disturbance years (no bleaching, no
major hurricanes, no disease outbreaks).

KEY CLAIMS RELEVANT TO OUR PAPER
=================================
1. "Once outplanted, corals behave just as wild colonies" (Abstract)
   - Origin-insensitivity claim for A. cervicornis at the outplant stage
   - Supported by: mean outplant survival 85.2% roughly matches reports
     for wild A. cervicornis in non-disturbance years

2. "Regional departures from the mean can be used as early warning signals"
   - Framework for site-specific or program-specific diagnostics

3. "No excess damage is caused to donor colonies"
   - <=10% biomass removal is demographically safe for large healthy donors
   - Pre-2023; all samples pre-heatwave

OUR ENGAGEMENT IN THE MANUSCRIPT
=================================
Introduction: invoke Schopmeyer's origin-insensitivity claim for
A. cervicornis as the sister-species hypothesis against which our
A. palmata origin-effect tests are framed.

Discussion: compare quantitatively to our meta-analytic A. palmata pool
(k=17, 22 effects; `06_analysis/output/expanded_meta_analysis_results.csv`):
  - Natural: 84.1% -> matches Schopmeyer outplant benchmark (85.2%)
  - Restoration: 74.5% -> falls in Schopmeyer "yellow" band
  - Natural-vs-restoration contrast: p=0.238, not significant
    -> partial support for origin-insensitivity at colony stage
  - Subtype decomposition reveals origin penalties hidden in pooled avg:
    - Nursery outplants: 59.8% (A. palmata) -> Schopmeyer "red"
    - Outplanted colonies: 65.2% -> Schopmeyer "red"
  - Recruit stage (Chamberland, Mendoza-Quiroz, Fundemar):
    -> order-of-magnitude origin effects (0.5% ex-situ vs 3.4-6.9% outplant)
    -> Schopmeyer benchmarks do NOT apply at this life stage because
       A. cervicornis restoration bypasses ex-situ larval culture

Take-home: Schopmeyer benchmarks are a reasonable target for A. palmata
ONCE colonies reach SC2+ and are outplanted, but origin penalties dominate
at earlier stages and within-restoration subtypes. Size- and stage-resolved
benchmarks are needed for A. palmata.

CAVEATS FOR USE
===============
- All data 2010-2014, pre-2023 heatwave
- "Normal conditions" filter excludes disturbance years entirely; our
  synthesis includes and explicitly models disturbance
- Florida + Puerto Rico only; doesn't span the Caribbean range
- A. cervicornis has branching morphology and modular fragment architecture
  distinct from A. palmata's plate/branch form; origin effects may differ
  for mechanistic reasons (wound closure rates, partial mortality dynamics)
- Benchmarks are frequentist means with regional SDs, not model-based
  size-resolved predictions - direct comparison at size-class level is not
  possible from this paper alone
