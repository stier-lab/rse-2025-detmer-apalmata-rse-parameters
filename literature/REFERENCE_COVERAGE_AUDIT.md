# *Acropora palmata* reference coverage audit

Date: 2026-10-01

This audit compares the manuscript's in-text citations with the project library. It distinguishes papers that support the manuscript's argument from papers that were retained as source data or screened but were not appropriate as supporting citations.

## Newly embedded anchor studies

| Theme | Studies now cited | Role in the manuscript |
|---|---|---|
| Disease and colony loss | Gladfelter (1982); Lentz et al. (2011); Williams and Miller (2012) | Establish that disease and mortality processes are integral to *A. palmata* demography, rather than unmodeled background noise. |
| Disturbance and thermal bottlenecks | Rogers and Muller (2012); Randall and Szmant (2009) | Motivate treatment of disturbance history as biological context across life stages. |
| Population dynamics and restoration heterogeneity | Lirman (2003); Vardi et al. (2012); Forrester et al. (2012); Banister et al. (2024) | Frame why fragment provenance, handling, site, and disturbance cannot be collapsed into a single restoration effect. |
| Sexual recruitment | Chamberland et al. (2015); Mendoza Quiroz et al. (2023); Ritson-Williams et al. (2013, 2020) | Bound the recruitment-free model and explain why early recruitment rates cannot be inferred from established outplants. |

## Retained as data sources or contextual records

The manuscript already cites its longitudinal source studies in the study-selection section and/or tables, including Rogers et al. (1982), Bruckner and Bruckner (2001), Ortiz Prosper (2005), Garrison and Ward (2008), Williams and Miller (2010), Vardi (2011), Rogers and Muller (2012), Forrester et al. (2014), Maurer et al. (2022), Rosales et al. (2024), and Ramos Romero et al. (2025). These records support the synthesis directly and are not merely background citations.

## Library studies not embedded in prose

Several library records remain uncited because they are cross-sectional status surveys, molecular or symbiont studies, reef-framework or fish-habitat studies, or methods records outside the manuscript's demographic argument. They remain useful for future analyses but would broaden this paper without improving support for its central claims. The audit deliberately does not treat a library-wide citation dump as comprehensive scholarship.
